Abstract

Which content pages should a team review first when time is limited? This study compares a transparent rule-based baseline with a Random Forest classifier using an anonymized 30,000-page starter dataset and a client-grouped holdout. The Random Forest measured Precision@10, Precision@50, and Precision@100 of 0.50, 0.64, and 0.61, compared with 0.30 at all three cutoffs for the baseline. A random-row split produced higher results, but possible overlap between clients makes it an optimistic sensitivity reference rather than the primary estimate. The resulting queue is intended for human-review prioritization, not as proof of future decline or evidence that refreshing a page causes better search performance.

1. Introduction and problem statement

Content teams may manage many pages but have limited time to investigate them. The practical question is which pages deserve attention first for refreshing, expansion, protection, pruning, or monitoring.

Research question: Which content pages should be reviewed first using measurable search visibility, engagement, freshness, and content characteristics?

The unit of analysis is one content page. The intended output is a ranked review queue containing a model-based decline probability, a reason code, and a suggested review action.

A fixed rule can identify obvious cases, such as older pages with high search visibility. A Random Forest can combine more signals and capture nonlinear relationships that a simple rule may miss. The goal is to support editorial review, not to automate editorial decisions.

2. Data and data safety

Dataset and scope

The FlyRank ML Internship describes a larger search-intelligence warehouse of approximately 79 million rows and provides a smaller anonymized starter dataset. The model results reported here use the 30,000-page starter dataset with 44 columns; the Random Forest was not trained on the full warehouse.

Earlier data-contract work examined fact_content_daily_performance, with dim_content used when content metadata was required. That work checked the February 1–28, 2026 feature window with a decision cutoff at the end of February. The warehouse checks covered 7,351,108 daily-grain rows; GSC data was available for 2,621,783 of them. These checks are distinct from the starter-dataset model evaluation.

Model features

The 12 inputs were days_since_last_update, impressions_90d, clicks_90d, sessions_90d, ctr, avg_position, engagement_rate, scroll_rate, content_age_days, word_count, search_volume, and cpc.

Target and exclusions

The target proxy was is_declining_label = (trend_direction == "down"). This is a dataset-derived label, not a guaranteed future outcome. trend_direction and trend_pct were excluded from model inputs because they are outcome-derived. The target itself was excluded from features. Pseudonymous content_id and client_id were not model inputs; client IDs were used only for grouped validation.

Public-safety note: this page reports aggregate results and methodology. It does not publish client names, private search queries, or identifying client-page URLs.

3. Methodology

Transparent baseline

The Week-4 baseline combined freshness and search visibility. Staleness contributed 0 points for up to 90 days, 1 point for 91–180 days, and 2 points for more than 180 days. Visibility contributed 0 points for fewer than 250 impressions, 1 point for 250–499, and 2 points for at least 500 impressions. The sum ranged from 0 to 4; scores of at least 3 received the Refresh action, while the others received Monitor.

Random Forest

The classifier used 300 trees, random_state=42, min_samples_leaf=5, and median imputation. It combined the 12 listed features to estimate the probability associated with the declining-label proxy.

The primary evaluation grouped pages by client so a client's pages remained in one partition. Precision@K was the main metric because the intended use is prioritizing a limited number of pages for review.

Leakage audit

The final audit excluded trend_direction, trend_pct, is_declining_label, content_id, and client_id from model inputs. The audit passed. An earlier deliberate leakage experiment produced an artificially perfect score of 1.0000 after a future outcome-derived feature was introduced, demonstrating why such fields must be excluded.

Honest interpretation: the Random Forest measured higher Precision@K than the baseline on this holdout. These observations do not prove future performance, statistical significance, or causal impact.

5. Interpretation

Permutation importance provided a diagnostic view of the model's inputs. The largest mean values were for impressions_90d (0.0605), avg_position (0.0350), content_age_days (0.0114), clicks_90d (0.0100), and ctr (0.0095).

These are model diagnostics, not causal effects. Negative permutation importance for days_since_last_update means that permuting the feature did not reduce average-precision performance on this evaluation set; it does not mean freshness causes worse outcomes.

6. Limitations and honest framing

  • Dataset scope: model results use the 30,000-page starter dataset, not the full approximately 79-million-row warehouse.
  • Proxy target: the declining label is dataset-derived and is not a guaranteed future outcome.
  • Generalization: the primary evaluation uses one holdout containing seven test clients; performance across all clients and future periods is not established.
  • Metric scope: Precision@K does not measure business value or editorial quality directly.
  • No causal evidence: no randomized refresh intervention was conducted. The analysis cannot establish that refreshing a page causes improved search performance.
  • Human review: high-ranked pages may not require action, and lower-ranked pages may still be important.

The model must not autonomously publish, rewrite, delete, redirect, or otherwise modify content.

7. Ranked recommendations: Content action playbook

The queue is designed to help content teams decide which pages to investigate first. It is not an automatic content-change system.

Priority levels

PriorityDecline probabilityRecommended next step
Priority review≥ 0.70Investigate first; verify signals and page context.
Review≥ 0.50 and < 0.70Investigate after the priority queue.
Monitor< 0.50Continue monitoring unless other evidence appears.

These thresholds are operational prioritization choices, not calibrated guarantees of future decline.

Reason codes

Reason codeTriggerSuggested action
STALE_HIGH_VISIBILITYMore than 180 days since update and at least 500 impressionsPriority refresh review
STALE_PAGEMore than 180 days since updateInvestigate whether a refresh is appropriate
HIGH_VISIBILITY_REVIEWAt least 500 impressions and elevated model rankingReview search performance and intent alignment
AGING_PAGEMore than 90 days since updateMonitor or investigate freshness
MONITORNo higher-priority rule appliesContinue monitoring

Human-review workflow

Rank → investigate → validate context → decide → act → monitor

Before acting, a reviewer should verify search intent, factual accuracy, business importance, seasonality, recent changes, ranking context, and whether a page is strategically protected.

Cost, value, and monitoring

The expected value is better use of limited reviewer time. Costs include reviewing pages that do not need action and potentially missing lower-ranked pages that do. Monitor data quality, feature drift, ranking concentration, and Precision@K on newly labeled outcomes. Material changes or new labels should trigger fresh grouped validation.

What must not be automated

Do not autonomously publish or rewrite content, delete pages, change redirects or canonicals, make factual or editorial decisions, or treat a score as proof that refreshing will improve performance.
